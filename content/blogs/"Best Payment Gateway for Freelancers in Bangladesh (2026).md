# Best Payment Method for Freelancers (2026): WorldFirst vs Elevate Pay vs Nsave

## What "Best Payment Gateway" Actually Means for a Freelancer

If you are a freelancer in Bangladesh, Pakistan, and other Southest Asian country you already know the real work doesn't end when you deliver the project. The second problem starts right after your client pays you, how do you actually bring that money home without losing a big chunk of it. A payment gateway for a freelancer is not a checkout button like Stripe, it's the account that receives your Fiverr or Upwork payout, holds it in USD, and lets you either spend it directly or convert it to BDT at a fair rate. That's the whole game.

Only Bangladesh has over 700,000 active freelancers as of 2026, and the country consistently ranks among the top 5 to 10 nations globally on platforms like Fiverr and Upwork. The IT and ITES export earnings from Bangladesh have crossed $1.4 billion annually. Yet the payment infrastructure for bringing that money home has always been the weakest link in the chain.

### The Pain Freelancers in Bangladesh Have Been Taking for Years

For a long time freelancers in Bangladesh and the wider South Asian region had almost no good option. If you wanted to bring your dollar earning home as remittance the process was unclear, slow, and the incentive on remittance was never explained properly by the bank. Global platforms like Payoneer and Wise did exist and they solved the basic problem of receiving money, but the fees for every single feature and the exchange rate they gave was honestly terrible compared to what the mid-market rate actually was.

Another pain is your local bank's forex fees, international transaction cost and dual currency card. These cards come with many restrictions, and has yearly fees, and most bank employees are not even familiar with how ERQ (Exporters' Retention Quota) accounts work. Under Bangladesh Bank guidelines, freelancers classified as IT/ITES exporters can retain up to 30 to 50 (for it exporter) percent of their foreign currency earnings in an ERQ account and spend it directly in USD for business purposes like software subscriptions, hosting, and international tools. The problem is the paperwork is heavy, the process is bureaucratic, and bank employees at the branch level often don't understand the procedure. Some banks do offer a system where you can bring money in USD and spend directly from a card linked to an ERQ account, but the yearly fee and transaction fee on those is high that most freelancers just give up and use whatever their bank offers, even when it's a bad deal.

What freelancers actually need is a smooth USD spending card that works everywhere without this drama, and that's exactly the gap WorldFirst, Elevate Pay, and nsave are trying to fill in 2026.

### The Bangladesh Government Remittance Incentive: Free Extra Money Many Freelancers Don't Know About

The Bangladesh government offers a **2.5% cash incentive on all inward remittances** through legal channels. This means for every $1,000 you withdraw to your Bangladeshi bank or bKash, you get an extra $25 equivalent in BDT on top of whatever the exchange rate gives you. This incentive was introduced to encourage freelancers and overseas workers to use formal banking channels instead of the informal market.

Some of the newer platforms like Elevate Pay and nsave automatically apply this incentive during withdrawal, which is a massive advantage. If you're withdrawing through a platform that doesn't apply it automatically, you need to make sure your bank is crediting it, because many banks in Bangladesh don't explain this properly or apply it late.

### Tax Exemption for IT Freelancers in Bangladesh

Here's another thing most Bangladeshi freelancers don't know or don't take advantage of properly: the Bangladesh government has extended a **complete tax exemption on IT and ITES export income until June 2027 (FY 2026-27)**. This means if you're earning from freelancing in IT related fields like:

Your income is tax exempt. You still need a TIN (Tax Identification Number) for banking purposes and you should still file your tax return even if the income is exempt, but you won't owe tax on your freelance earnings. This is a huge incentive that makes Bangladesh one of the best countries in the world for freelancers from a tax perspective right now.

## Payoneer vs Wise: The Companies That Partially Solved the Problem

Before we get into the newer names let's talk about the two platforms almost every Bangladeshi freelancer has already used or at least heard of.

### [Payoneer](https://payoneer.com): The Old Reliable With an Old Price Tag

Payoneer is still the Mandatory payment gateway among freelancers in Southest Asia and Bangladesh mainly because Fiverr made it the default option for years and so many emloyeer also use Payoneer. It's cheap to receive from those platforms if you're a "Payoneer to Payoneer" transfer, but the moment you step outside that lane the fees start adding up fast.

Here's what Payoneer actually costs you in 2026:

| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Receiving from Fiverr/Upwork (Payoneer to Payoneer) | 0% to 1% |
| Receiving from non-Payoneer source (credit/debit card) | Up to 3.0% to 3.2% plus flat fee |
| Receiving via bank transfer/ACH | Up to 1% |
| Sending to non-Payoneer bank account | 1% to 4% |
| Withdrawal to local bank (BDT) | ~$1.50 per withdrawal plus FX markup |
| Payoneer Commercial Mastercard annual fee | $29.95 |
| ATM withdrawal per transaction | $3.15 |
| FX markup on exchange rate | 1% to 2% over mid-market rate |
| Currency conversion fee | Up to 3.5% above mid-market in some cases |
| Inactivity fee (12 months no activity) | Applicable |

The exchange rate is where Payoneer really loses freelancers money. It's not the mid-market rate, there's a 1 to 2 percent markup baked in, and over a year of monthly withdrawals that markup adds up to real money you'll never see again. If you're withdrawing $1,000 a month through Payoneer, that hidden FX markup alone can cost you $120 to $240 a year, on top of all the other fees. Payoneer is not a bad platform, it's just an old one built for a time when freelancers didn't have better options.

### [Wise](https://wise.com): Great Rate, But Barely Usable From Bangladesh Now

Wise is known worldwide for its transparent pricing and near mid-market exchange rate, and if you could use it properly it would honestly beat most competitors on rate alone. The problem is as of 2026 Wise has restricted functionality for Bangladeshi residents in a very significant way:

- **Cannot** open a full multi-currency account from Bangladesh
- **Cannot** order a Wise debit card
- **Cannot** get USD, GBP, or EUR account details to receive payments directly
- **Can** send BDT out to a Bangladeshi bank account, bKash, or Nagad (from a Wise account funded from another country)

So Wise can be used to send money *to* Bangladesh, but not to receive money *from* a client or hold a multi-currency balance as a Bangladeshi resident. For receiving Fiverr and Upwork payments, Wise is basically off the table for a Bangladeshi freelancer right now, no matter how good the rate used to be.

This is exactly why WorldFirst, Elevate Pay, and nsave have grown so fast in the last year or two. They stepped in to solve the exact gap Payoneer and Wise left open.

- **WorldFirst + Elevate Pay:** Use WorldFirst to receive marketplace payouts in multiple currencies, hold your funds safely with tier-one banks, and spend with virtual cards. Use Elevate Pay for instant free bKash withdrawal when you need BDT fast, and for receiving ACH payments from direct US clients. Move money between them as needed.

- **WorldFirst + nsave:** Use WorldFirst for receiving and security, nsave for USD spending card and earning interest on the portion of your balance you don't need immediately.

- **Elevate Pay + nsave:** Use Elevate Pay for receiving and fast bKash withdrawal, nsave for the virtual Visa card and interest earning. This combination works if most of your income comes through marketplaces and you don't need 15+ currencies.

The key principle is: **receive through the platform with the best integration for your income source, hold in the platform with the best security, spend through the platform with the best card, and withdraw through the platform with the fastest BDT access.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Payoneer still worth using in 2026?**

Payoneer still works fine if you're only ever moving money Payoneer to Payoneer (has cost for foreign outbound country), but the moment you step outside that lane the fees and exchange rate markup start costing you real money. The $29.95 annual card fee, the 1 to 2 percent FX markup, the up to 3.2% receiving fee from non-Payoneer sources, all of it adds up. Most freelancers now use it as a backup, not their main account. If you're already on Payoneer and comfortable, you don't need to switch overnight, but you should at least open one of these three accounts and compare the rate yourself on your next withdrawal.

**Can I still use Wise in Bangladesh?**

You can send BDT out through Wise, but as of 2026 you cannot open a full multi-currency account, get a Wise card, or receive USD account details as a Bangladeshi resident. For receiving Fiverr or Upwork payments, Wise is no longer a practical option here. If you have a friend or family member abroad who needs to send you money, Wise is still good for that specific direction of transfer.

**Which platform has the best exchange rate for freelancers in Bangladesh?**

nsave and Elevate Pay generally quote a better FX rate than Payoneer, Wise, Remitly, Airtm, and Xoom for BDT conversions. WorldFirst's rate is also very competitive, especially for larger amounts where they offer tighter spreads. Always check the live rate at the time of withdrawal since these change daily. The 2.5% government remittance incentive (applied automatically by Elevate Pay and nsave) also makes a meaningful difference.

**Is nsave safe to use for large amounts?**

nsave is a Y Combinator backed company and does use FCA-regulated infrastructure through Keel Money, but it does not hold a top-tier license of its own the way WorldFirst does. There's also no FDIC insurance like Elevate Pay offers. Many freelancers use it for day-to-day USD spending and the virtual card, and keep larger long-term holdings in a more heavily regulated account like WorldFirst, But for working capital and spending, it's fine.

**Can I receive PayPal payments in Bangladesh?**

PayPal has very limited functionality in Bangladesh, mostly for sending, not receiving. For practical purposes, PayPal is not an option for receiving freelance payments as a Bangladeshi resident. This is exactly why platforms like WorldFirst, Elevate Pay, and nsave exist.

**What is an ERQ account and should I get one?**

ERQ (Exporters' Retention Quota) is a Bangladesh Bank provision that lets IT/ITES exporters retain 30 to 50 percent of their foreign earnings in a USD account at their local bank. In theory this is useful for spending in USD without conversion. In practice, the bank-level implementation is often terrible, with high annual fees, transaction fees, unfamiliar branch staff, and heavy paperwork. If your bank makes it easy and cheap, go for it. But for most freelancers, a WorldFirst World Card or nsave Visa card is a much simpler way to spend in USD directly.

**Do I need to pay tax on my freelance income in Bangladesh?**

As of 2026, IT and ITES export income is fully tax exempt in Bangladesh until June 2027 (FY 2026-27). You should still get a TIN and file your returns even if the income is exempt, because you'll need the TIN for banking and large transactions anyway. Consult with a tax professional for your specific situation.
